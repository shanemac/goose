Meet Goose

Goose is your co-founder. It learns what you're great at and does everything else, so you stay connected to your users and focused on what they actually say, not what you think.

Start with Goose

More docs and more flowcharts won't make your startup work anymore. An agent can make one in five seconds. You can now produce more plans in a week than a founder could in a year, five years ago. A plan you can rewrite every day doesn't tell you much. And a plan never gives you honest feedback.

The biggest trap used to be writing a plan and asking people you know what they think. Their incentive is to like it. That isn't feedback.

The new loop is plan, product, feedback, and it can happen in real time. What matters is honest feedback on a real product: watching how people actually use it, and seeing whether it has the magic spark. And never being afraid to throw things out, start over, and rebuild until you find the thing, instead of polishing something people don't really want.

It's always a search for the truth, and not fooling ourselves. A founder's confidence is the fastest path to delusion. Goose's job is to find what's true, because that's the only way to find real growth.

Anyone can now build almost anything, alone, in private, talking to an AI.

That's new, and it's a trap. It fails you in two ways.

You get lonely. You talk to agents all day and stop talking to people. The agent always agrees with you. It never churns, never pays, and never tells you the thing is confusing.

You overbuild. When building is nearly free, you build everything, long before you learn whether anyone wants any of it.

So here's the whole idea. When you can build everything, the only thing that matters is not building the wrong thing. Agents can talk to users now, and they can build the systems that collect feedback. What they can't do is care whether you're actually connected to the people you serve, or stop you from mistaking your own opinions for what users want. That part is yours, and Goose won't let you skip it.

Then build with a simple rule borrowed from Mark Pincus: proven, better, new. Start from something people already do and want. Make it clearly better. Add one new thing. Most of what you'd build beyond that is a guess.

Goose is a co-founder for that. It's a Grok Bot whose core job is to keep you connected to real people and honest about what they say. Ship to production. Build relationships with users. Capture every piece of feedback. Cut features. Find where there's real pull.

At its core, Goose keeps all your ideas, projects, and teams of people and agents moving forward. And it's the ultimate clarifier. When things get fuzzy, it asks what we're actually doing, who owns it, and what happens next, until the answer fits in one sentence.

And it does everything else. Not advice about the work, the actual work. A good partner says "I'll just do it." So does Goose.

As your CTO, it builds. It sets up the repo, picks the architecture, writes the code, ships prototypes, deploys to production, wires up analytics and feedback capture, and fixes what breaks.

As your chief of staff, it keeps track of everything. It owns your mission, values, and strategy docs, runs the daily, weekly, and monthly rituals, preps you for meetings, follows up on what you said you'd do, and keeps your group chats on the same page.

As your COO, it runs the operation. It builds the systems that reach users and sort their feedback, sets up the tools and processes, drafts the outreach, and when the company needs more hands, it spins up new Grok Bots for specific jobs and manages them all.

  • Every weekday morning, it asks what you're shipping today and who will see it.
  • Every Friday, it asks what shipped, how many users you connected with, what the feedback says, where the pull is, and what to cut.
  • Every month, it checks whether you're default alive and whether this is still the mission.
  • It owns your mission, values, and strategy, so you never have to sit down and write them.

Goose is named for geese flying in a V: each bird rides the lift of the one ahead, they honk to keep each other going, and they take turns at the front, so the flock flies farther than any one bird could.

Goose turns that into one number, the Goose Score: a 1 to 10 grade for your startup, built from how fast you're growing, whether people come back, how often you ship, and how many real people you heard from this week.

The numbers behind it are yours. Goose works with you to pick the metrics that feed each part, because growth, love, and shipping look different for every product. A marketplace might count completed orders, and a developer tool might count weekly deploys.

The score tells you what to do next. A low score means change something big, and a high score means keep pushing, so you don't spend months iterating on something that isn't working.

  • 1–3Not working. Throw it out and try something radically different: a new customer, a new problem, or a new product.
  • 4–5Flat. This is where people get stuck making small fixes. Make one big change to who it's for, the core loop, or how you describe it.
  • 6–7Something's there. Find out what the people who love it love. Cut everything else and make that part sharper.
  • 8–9Working. Keep shipping fast and improving it, and push on growth.
  • 9+Great. This is the magic spark. Pour fuel on it.

If you're in the same band three weeks in a row, make a bigger change.

Under all of it is one habit: build a simpler version and get feedback. On the product and on how you describe it. Prototypes, not plans. Some progress every week.

  • Daily: ship something, and capture what people said.
  • Weekly: put a simpler version in front of real users.
  • Monthly: check you're default alive, and that this is still the mission.

Goose is an agent too, so it's built to never become your substitute for real people. It can reach users, gather feedback, and sort it for you, but every conversation with it ends pointed at a human relationship.

Everything in the open. These are the actual files Goose runs on. Read them, copy them, or argue with them.

  • AGENT.md: who Goose is.
  • SOUL.md: how it thinks and behaves.
  • MISSION.md: the thesis and the one thing we always do.
  • VALUES.md: what we believe.
  • RESPONSIBILITIES.md: who owns what.
  • RITUALS.md: daily, weekly, monthly, quarterly.
  • ROUTINES.md: the scheduled check-ins.
  • GETTING-STARTED.md: the first conversation.
  • TEMPLATES.md: the company folder it keeps.

Use Goose in any agent. Already have an agent you love? Plug Goose into it. Your agent gets Goose's soul, its rituals, and a quick check that asks what's live, who used it, and what to cut.

Resources and skills. Ask your agent to read these sites and skills to get these powers and thinking.

  • Impeccable: a design skill for great product design and management.
  • StartupKit: GTM integrations to drive your growth.
  • Lev: brainstorm new ideas, and have your Grok Bot manage Lev for you.
  • The Mom Test: Rob Fitzpatrick's guide to talking with customers so they can't just be nice to you.
  • Superhuman's product-market fit engine: Rahul Vohra's "how disappointed would you be without it?" survey and its 40% bar, a real way to measure the magic spark.
  • Superpowers: Jesse Vincent's skill pack that makes coding agents work like senior engineers: plan small, test, ship.
  • Anthropic's skills library: the official set of agent skills, including the frontend design skill.
  • PostHog: product analytics, session replays, and surveys in one place, so your agent can see how people actually use the product.
  • YC Library: Y Combinator's advice for founders, in one place.
  • Paul Graham's essays: the source material behind most of Goose's soul.
  • The Linear Method: a short guide to building with momentum and taste.
