Goose

Goose is your co-founder. It learns what you're great at and does everything else, so you stay connected to your users and focused on what they actually say, not what you think.

Anyone can now build almost anything, alone, in private, talking to an AI.

That's new, and it's a trap. It fails you in two ways.

You get lonely. You talk to agents all day and stop talking to people. The agent always agrees with you. It never churns, never pays, and never tells you the thing is confusing.

You overbuild. When building is nearly free, you build everything, long before you learn whether anyone wants any of it.

So here's the whole idea. When you can build everything, the only thing that matters is not building the wrong thing. Agents can talk to users now, and they can build the systems that collect feedback. What they can't do is care whether you're actually connected to the people you serve, or stop you from mistaking your own opinions for what users want. That part is yours, and Goose won't let you skip it.

Then build with a simple rule borrowed from Mark Pincus: proven, better, new. Start from something people already do and want. Make it clearly better. Add one new thing. Most of what you'd build beyond that is a guess.

Goose is a co-founder for that. It's a Grok Bot whose core job is to keep you connected to real people and honest about what they say. Ship to production. Build relationships with users. Capture every piece of feedback. Cut features. Find where there's real pull.

And it does everything else. Not advice about the work, the actual work. A good partner says "I'll just do it." So does Goose.

As your CTO, it builds. It sets up the repo, picks the architecture, writes the code, ships prototypes, deploys to production, wires up analytics and feedback capture, and fixes what breaks.

As your chief of staff, it keeps track of everything. It owns your mission, values, and strategy docs, runs the daily, weekly, and monthly rituals, preps you for meetings, follows up on what you said you'd do, and keeps your group chats on the same page.

As your COO, it runs the operation. It builds the systems that reach users and sort their feedback, sets up the tools and processes, drafts the outreach, and when the company needs more hands, it spins up new Grok Bots for specific jobs and manages them all.

  • Every weekday morning, it asks what you're shipping today and who will see it.
  • Every Friday, it asks what shipped, how many users you connected with, what the feedback says, where the pull is, and what to cut.
  • Every month, it checks whether you're default alive and whether this is still the mission.
  • It owns your mission, values, and strategy, so you never have to sit down and write them.

Under all of it is one habit: build a simpler version and get feedback. On the product and on how you describe it. Prototypes, not plans. Some progress every week.

  • Daily: ship something, and capture what people said.
  • Weekly: put a simpler version in front of real users.
  • Monthly: check you're default alive, and that this is still the mission.

It's named for the geese that fly in a V. They take turns at the front, and the ones behind honk to keep the leader going.

Goose is an agent too, so it's built to never become your substitute for real people. It can reach users, gather feedback, and sort it for you, but every conversation with it ends pointed at a human relationship.
